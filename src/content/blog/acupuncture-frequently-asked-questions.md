## Acupuncture vs. dry needling: what is the difference?

This is one of the most common questions I get. Both use thin needles, and there is overlap in technique. Acupuncture is a full system of care practiced by licensed acupuncturists with extensive training in needling and in whole-person assessment. Dry needling is typically taught as a shorter weekend course focused on muscle trigger points. **Acupuncture treats all of you. Dry needling treats a symptom only.**

What most patients find is that acupuncture feels more gentle and relaxing, and addresses both the symptom causing the trouble and the root cause from where it comes. Dry needling tends to be more painful and treats a local symptom with relief, but perhaps not always lasting results.

## What should I expect at my first visit?

- **A conversation.** We talk about your pain and other issues, your history, your daily life and your goals.
- **An exam.** I look at how you move and where you are tight or tender.
- **Treatment.** Needles are placed, sometimes with electro-acupuncture or cupping, and you rest for about 20 to 30 minutes.
- **A plan.** We decide how often to come in and how to measure progress. Many people with ongoing pain start with a short series of visits once weekly for 3–4 weeks, and then we stretch sessions out to make sure your body can hold the healing and new pattern.

## Does acupuncture hurt?

Most people feel very little when the needles go in. You may feel a dull ache, warmth or heaviness once they are placed. We are in constant contact about the needles, and I will adjust anything that is uncomfortable for your comfort and relaxation.

## How many sessions will I need?

It depends on what you are dealing with and how long you have had it. We will talk about a realistic plan in a free phone consult, and again at your first visit. Typically, a course of treatment is between 6–10 visits.

## Is it safe?

In the hands of a licensed practitioner using sterile single-use needles, acupuncture has a very good safety record. Tell me if you take blood thinners, are pregnant, or have a pacemaker, because that affects how I treat you, especially with electro-acupuncture.

## Can I combine acupuncture with physical therapy or chiropractic care?

Yes. Many patients do. Let me know what else you are doing so we can work together. Other members of your care team also affect the frequency of visits I recommend.

## What should I wear?

Loose, comfortable clothing that can easily be lifted above your knees and elbows is best. In some cases, you may need to remove clothing for access to shoulders, hips, glutes or other areas. You'll be covered and draped for comfort and privacy.
