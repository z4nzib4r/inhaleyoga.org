## What is acupuncture, in plain language?

Acupuncture uses very thin, sterile, single-use needles placed at specific points on the body. Most people are surprised by how gentle it feels. Many describe a mild ache, warmth or heaviness, and a lot of people relax enough to doze during treatment.

## What can acupuncture help with?

Research is strongest for musculoskeletal pain and certain headache conditions. Reviews and clinical guidelines support acupuncture as an option for:

- Chronic low back pain
- Neck and shoulder pain
- Knee osteoarthritis
- Tension-type headaches and migraine prevention
- Nausea related to chemotherapy or surgery

People also visit for stress, sleep trouble and general wellbeing. While I have a lot of success in my clinic helping people with these, the scientific evidence is more mixed, and I will always tell you honestly what to expect. Acupuncture works well alongside, not instead of, care from your doctor or physical therapist. I love being part of a care team.

## Sports and orthopedic acupuncture

If you are active, whether you run, lift, play rec-league sports, hike, or spend summers paddling the Ohio River, your tissues take a lot of repetitive load. Sports and orthopedic acupuncture focuses on the muscles, tendons and joints involved in your specific complaint, such as:

- Tight, painful shoulders and rotator cuff irritation
- Tennis and golfer's elbow
- Hip, IT band and hamstring tightness
- Runner's knee and shin pain
- Low back strain from lifting or long hours sitting or standing
- Sciatica-type leg pain

## Electro-acupuncture and motor-point technique

Electro-acupuncture connects a gentle electrical current to the needles. The sensation is a mild tapping or buzzing, and the current is adjusted to your comfort. Electrical stimulation is often used for stubborn muscle tension and chronic pain.

Motor-point technique targets the specific points where a muscle is most responsive, which can help a tight or guarded muscle release. These sessions are more nuanced and typically require skilled assessment as well as acupuncture and electrical stimulation.

## Cupping

Cupping uses suction on the skin over tight muscles to encourage circulation and ease tension. It can leave temporary round marks that fade in several days. They are not bruises from injury, and they are not painful. Cupping is often paired with acupuncture for neck, shoulder and back tightness, and my patients love the lasting effects.
